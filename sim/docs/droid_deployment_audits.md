# DROID deployment audits and Polymetis feasibility

Baseline: `experiment/isaacsim-fr3-droid` at `ca04a0f5795c47be61f6725ce7be20ef51cd5033`. Source pins: [DROID `33ae6a6`](https://github.com/droid-dataset/droid/tree/33ae6a67274f36d2e29525b86f23a56616ef43a7), its Fairo/Polymetis submodule [gitlink `0a01a7f`](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/tree/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2), and [OpenPI `15a9616`](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/tree/15a9616a00943ada6c20a0f158e3adb39df2ccac). These are source revisions, not proof of the configuration actually loaded on any physical robot.

## Action-execution audit

The pinned [OpenPI DROID example](https://github.com/Physical-Intelligence/openpi/blob/15a9616a00943ada6c20a0f158e3adb39df2ccac/examples/droid/main.py) clips each action coordinate to `[-1,1]`. DROID's [`RobotIKSolver.joint_velocity_to_delta`](https://github.com/droid-dataset/droid/blob/33ae6a67274f36d2e29525b86f23a56616ef43a7/droid/robot_ik/robot_ik_solver.py) scales it by 0.2 rad; its vector normalization is inactive after that clipping. [`FrankaRobot.create_action_dict` and `update_joints`](https://github.com/droid-dataset/droid/blob/33ae6a67274f36d2e29525b86f23a56616ef43a7/droid/franka/robot.py) reference fresh measured joint positions and send absolute position targets at 15 Hz in the nonblocking path:

`q_des[k] = q_measured[k] + 0.2 × clip(action[k, :7], -1, 1)`.

Our [`map_droid_reference_joint_action`](../../src/saps/policies/openpi_droid.py) and Isaac [`arm_target` / `execute_chunk`](../src/isaac_fr3/policy_execution.py) implement this high-level mapping. The pinned DROID launch uses Polymetis torque control; Isaac uses PhysX position drives, so matching targets do not establish matching low-level motion. In the recorded eight-action free-space replay, median realized/commanded joint displacement was **48.67% with Franka Hand** and **48.64% with Robotiq**. Maximum end-of-action joint error was **0.04038 rad**. No source-level arm action-mapping bug was found. Full per-action and per-joint values, TCP poses, source action and raw substeps are under [`outputs/isaac_droid_control_audit/`](../../outputs/isaac_droid_control_audit/).

The official example predicts 15 actions and executes eight at 15 Hz. It samples an observation before synchronous inference while the physical controller continues running. Our inference hold, paused Isaac physics, initial HOME differences, and eight-action execution horizon are deliberate experimental choices, **not identified bugs**. Gripper command dynamics also differ: DROID commands a width/speed/force through its gripper interface; Isaac's Franka fingers and Robotiq driver have their own actuation behavior.

## SIM-P9: PhysX drive response

Both baseline scenes loaded arm position drives with stiffness `3,437,746.75`, damping `343,774.6875`, and `D/K ≈ 0.100 s`. A stationary HOME step reached 63.2% of commanded displacement in **0.091–0.108 s** across joints/scenes; the baseline four-physics-step (66.7 ms) displacement was about **48.7%**. The simple overdamped response `1 − exp(−0.0667/0.100) ≈ 0.487` explains the dominant ratio. Halving damping increased the sampled median to about **73.5%**. Doubling arm effort limits left the sampled trajectories unchanged. The internal implicit-drive torque was **not directly observable**; neither zero external actuation effort nor projected joint force is a direct drive-torque measurement. The faster variant has **not** been shown to match physical DROID. Contact and solver effects, including a late Robotiq half-damping disturbance, remain incompletely identified. Raw trajectories, response times, conditions, and plots are under [`outputs/isaac_droid_drive_response/`](../../outputs/isaac_droid_drive_response/).

Both full audit archives are ignored generated outputs. **Retain or export them separately; this document does not archive their raw data.** Remaining deployment differences are low-level control and target delivery, gripper dynamics, unidentified physical controller settings/response, and simulation/contact fidelity. Neither audit establishes physical torque or grasp equivalence.

## Pinned Polymetis feasibility

The DROID [`launch_robot.sh`](https://github.com/droid-dataset/droid/blob/33ae6a67274f36d2e29525b86f23a56616ef43a7/droid/franka/launch_robot.sh) activates `polymetis-local` and launches `robot_client=franka_hardware`. In pinned Fairo, [`RobotInterface.start_cartesian_impedance`](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/polymetis/python/polymetis/robot_interface.py) constructs `HybridJointImpedanceControl` using default `Kq`, `Kqd`, `Kx`, and `Kxd`; `update_desired_joint_positions()` updates its `joint_pos_desired` parameter. Despite the latter method's docstring saying “joint impedance,” the source supports DROID's call sequence. The [hybrid policy](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/polymetis/python/torchcontrol/policies/impedance.py) initializes the reference from measured q and sends joint torques from joint PD plus Jacobian-projected Cartesian gains and inverse-dynamics feedforward. It does not implement a separate Cartesian pose-error target for this call sequence.

The pinned generic [hardware config](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/polymetis/conf/robot_client/franka_hardware.yaml) specifies 1 kHz, `Kq=[40,30,50,25,35,25,10]`, `Kqd=[4,6,5,5,3,2,1]`, `Kx=[750,750,750,15,15,15]`, `Kxd=[37,37,37,2,2,2]`, rate limiting, and a 100 Hz low-pass setting. [DROID's installation guide](https://github.com/droid-dataset/droid/blob/33ae6a67274f36d2e29525b86f23a56616ef43a7/docs/software-setup/host-installation.md) instructs operators to replace both hardware and robot-model YAML with robot-specific files. Thus actual physical gains, active filtering/rate limits, FR3 model, and achieved update rate cannot be recovered from the pin alone.

Pinned Fairo documents a local [PyBullet launch](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/docs/source/usage.md) and supplies `franka_sim.yaml` with complete simulator gains. Its `bullet_sim.yaml` references top-level gain keys absent from the plain launch config, so the documented `robot_client=bullet_sim` command needs configuration verification; `robot_client=franka_sim` is a source-backed candidate, **not a verified laptop command**. The bundled [robot model](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/polymetis/conf/robot_model/franka_panda.yaml) and URDF are Panda. No FR3 model/config is present in the pinned Fairo tree. A genuine FR3 comparison requires the external robot-specific configuration and model.

The `RobotInterface` methods can accept the same seven recorded absolute joint-target vectors in order. An action-level comparison instead requires applying the recorded normalized actions to fresh PyBullet measured q at each 15 Hz boundary, then holding the final target. Either replay needs an isolated client script; neither was executed. Panda and FR3 responses would not be directly comparable. The [state protobuf](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/polymetis/protos/polymetis.proto) and `get_robot_state()`/state log expose q, dq, computed torque, previous pre/post-safety torque, simulated motor torque, and external torque fields. The [PyBullet client](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/polymetis/python/polysim/grpc_sim_client.py) fills its torque fields from the environment. “Measured” simulated motor torque is the environment's applied command after clipping/gravity compensation, not an independent physical sensor reading.

The [source-only feasibility check](../../outputs/isaac_droid_polymetis_feasibility/source_check.json) passed the method/reference checks. At the time of that audit, host Python 3.12.3 lacked Polymetis, PyTorch, PyBullet, Hydra, OmegaConf, and gRPC, with no native server or Conda environment. Pinned [installation requirements](https://github.com/facebookresearch/fairo/blob/0a01a7fa7a7c65b2f9a3aebf5e79040940daf9d2/polymetis/docs/source/installation.md) called for isolated Python 3.8 and a native server build. That audit did not launch simulation. The subsequent [isolated Panda integration](../integrations/polymetis_pybullet/README.md) built the pinned server and passed a stationary target/hold test with a clean shutdown; it does not establish FR3 or physical DROID equivalence.
